# 02 - Industry Research: GX Management Practice at Established Operators (2023-2026)

## How to read this

Research was done by web search in September 2026. The sandbox's outbound proxy blocked direct page
reads for almost every operator and publisher site, so findings come from search-result snippets and
summaries of the cited pages rather than full-page reads. Each finding carries a tag:

- **[P]** primary source (operator policy page, publisher's own report page), seen via snippet. Likely accurate; exact wording and date not confirmed.
- **[S]** secondary or vendor source. Indicative, not a benchmark.
- **[U]** unverified: vendor marketing or unattributed. Do not quote to stakeholders without checking.

Numbers that are worth putting in front of the LUF management team should be spot-checked at the
source URL first. The Sources list is at the end.

## 1. Timetable construction

**Les Mills guidance, used by most licensed clubs worldwide** [P]
- Six core class genres drive about 81% of global group fitness attendance. Les Mills recommends roughly 70% of the timetable to one programme per core genre in peak and shoulder slots, 20-30% to three or four specialty formats, and about 10% to intro or special-population classes off-peak. (Source 6)
- Offer each core programme at least 2-3 times a week. "Don't offer a programme if you can't fill it 3x a week." Fewer, fuller classes build slots faster than a wide menu. (Source 6) This matches Kenny's own rule in the 2022 briefing notes almost word for word.
- Double peak capacity with 30-minute high-intensity formats; use virtual classes off-peak to protect a 60-80% studio-utilisation target without adding instructor cost. (Source 7) LUF already runs a Fitbox virtual studio, which the briefing notes say should complement the live schedule.
- Timetable decisions are made on class attendance and programme performance; when a class is replaced, the incumbent instructor is offered training in the new programme to keep the slot. Variety across a single peak slot matters because many members can only attend one time. (Source 8, May 2023)

**SC Fitness, Portugal, Les Mills case study, March 2026** [P]
- Group fitness is 44% of all club visits. Attendance is captured automatically (kiosk card-scan and app), not counted by hand. A weekly scorecard tracks total GX attendance, GX share of visits, average attendance per class, class performance against target, class ranking, and programme performance. Instructor pay is calculated from the scorecard. (Source 9)

**What the big chains publish about timetable methodology**: essentially nothing. Equinox, Life Time, Virgin Active, David Lloyd, Fitness First, PURE, Celebrity Fitness, Crunch, 24 Hour Fitness, Planet Fitness, Anytime, F45 and Orangetheory publish member-facing booking rules (section 5) but not their internal class-mix ratios, review cadence or cut thresholds. Virgin Active UK says timetables are "reviewed regularly at each club" [P]; a member petition against Les Mills class cuts there is a cautionary example of poor change communication [S].

**Review cadence and change management** [S, consistent across vendors]
- Quarterly review is the most common cadence. ABC Glofox advises locking a template for about 3 months before major changes unless a class is clearly failing, so new formats get enough data. (Sources 38, 39)
- Analyse 90 days of attendance by day and slot. Typical finding: 60-70% of attendance falls in 30-40% of slots. Peak windows are commonly 6-8am and 5-7pm weekdays, 8-11am weekends. (Source 37)
- Give members about 10 days' notice of schedule changes. (Source 39)

**Minimum attendance and cut rules**
- Breakeven headcount = (instructor pay + per-class operating cost) divided by revenue per attendee. Worked example: (75 + 25) / 25 = 4 attendees. [S] (Source 37)
- Keep a class if fill rate is 50% or higher and trending up over a 6-8 week evaluation window; cut or move a class stuck at 35% after 8 weeks. Portfolio target: 75% average fill. [S] (Source 37)
- Published cancellation thresholds come from small operators, not chains: fewer than 5 participants for 3 consecutive weeks (Georgetown University), fewer than 3 with 4 hours' notice (an independent studio). [P, small operators] (Source 57)
- LUF's poster rule of "2 or fewer bookings = class cancelled" is at the aggressive end but consistent with the breakeven logic above.

**GX participation and retention (all Les Mills-commissioned, correlational)** [P]
- Les Mills class attendees visit 65% more often, stay 39% longer, are 88% more likely to still be active at 12 months, and have 27% higher lifetime value than other members. (Source 1)
- Members doing 3+ classes a week stay 50% longer, roughly 9.8 extra months of dues; average tenure 35.3 months. (Sources 4, 5)
- Group exercisers attending once a week are 20% more likely to be loyal than gym-floor-only members attending three times a week. 48% of 3x/week GX members are NPS promoters vs 36% of infrequent attendees. (Source 4)
- Village Gym (29 UK clubs): after raising instructor training and pay, class attendance rose 40% and monthly retention 5-10%. (Source 11)
- Caveat: no independent, non-Les Mills evidence was found. Self-selection of engaged members into GX is likely. Treat these as directional.

**Other industry reports**
- Les Mills 2026 Global Fitness Report (n over 10,000, 5 continents): 45% of Gen Z and Millennials seek group workouts; only 10% prefer an AI-created workout to a human-led one; 52% lean human-first. [P] (Sources 2, 3)
- ABC Fitness Wellness Watch year-end 2025 (40M members, 30,000 facilities): check-ins up 5% year on year; 57% of active consumers seek "connection"; Gen X and Boomers prefer smaller instructor-led groups. [P] (Sources 19, 20)
- Mindbody 2025 State of the Industry (about 1,421 operators): operators investing in AI are more likely to expand; 1 in 4 say AI makes staff more efficient; only 4% cut headcount; 56% cite personalised outreach as their top retention tactic. [P] (Source 21)
- No ClubIntel GX-specific report for 2024-2026 surfaced.

## 3. Instructor management

**Recruitment** [P]
- Les Mills "Nurturing the Next Gen" (Sept 2024, n=2,500 young people): top barriers to becoming an instructor are lack of a mentor and lack of confidence; 73% of prospects are already gym members; 69% of hot prospects want a clear progression pathway. Recruit from your own classes. (Source 12) This directly supports the JD's "recruit programme mentors" duty.
- Auditions are standard. Equinox requires a recognised GFI certification plus CPR/AED and runs a 3-week paid mentorship for cycling instructors. (Source 45) 42% of UK gyms struggle to fill instructor roles. [S] (Source 60)

**Onboarding** [S, consistent across ClubReady, Zen Planner, ChalkIt Pro]
- 30/60/90-day plan with a buddy or mentor and a weekly 30-minute check-in. Attend 15-20 classes as a participant in the first month; team-teach first classes; observe 1-2 classes a week during onboarding, then at least quarterly. (Source 46)

**Audits and evaluation**
- Athletic Business: annual full-class evaluation plus 10-minute spot evaluations; a 1-5 Likert rubric across class components and instruction skills (cueing, transitions, engagement, moving around the room), with written standards per score level. [P] (Source 43)
- Les Mills Qualifications assess across 5 Key Elements: Choreography, Technique, Coaching, Connection, Performance, producing a personal development plan. [P] (Source 13)
- HFA 24 best practices: a documented instructor code-of-conduct SOP with signed acknowledgement, equipment and tech training, modification competence, current certification and CEUs, and a communication system for announcements. [P] (Source 18)
- Post-class member ratings aggregated into instructor scorecards exist as vendor products (FitGrid, Hapana, WodGuru). Vendor response-rate claims of 55-65% are [U].

Comparison with LUF's QCC: LUF's 20-point binary checklist is heavier on logistics (12 of 20 items are pre- and post-class) than the Athletic Business or Les Mills rubrics, which weight coaching quality on a graded scale. The proposal recommends keeping the checklist but adding a 1-5 grade on the six "during class" items so the score discriminates between a competent and an excellent instructor.

**Substitution and cover**
- Dedicated tools: GroupEx PRO (owned by Daxko since 2019): sub requests, one-click approval, 24-hour reminders, attendance reporting, payroll by custom rate. NetGym: skills- and location-matched sub routing. ClassSub: closed trusted-instructor network. [P/S] (Sources 47-49)
- No published benchmark for an acceptable sub rate. Advice is consistent: a written sub policy, certification checks on subs, and showing instructors their own sub frequency for accountability. [S]

**Pay models** [P/S]
- US: mostly per class, USD 20-50 entry and 75+ experienced; a hybrid model (base plus USD 5-10 per head above a threshold) grew in 2024. Equinox cycling: USD 60-80 per class. (Sources 44, 45)
- SC Fitness pays from the attendance scorecard. Les Mills advocates linking pay to qualifications and results. (Source 9)
- Les Mills: members are often more loyal to their instructor than to the facility; instructor pay "has barely increased in 20 years". (Source 11)
- No per-class GX rate data for Malaysia, Singapore or Hong Kong was found. [gap]

Relevance to LUF: the booking-based pay model announced in the 2022 notes is exactly the hybrid model the industry moved to in 2024. The agent's payroll module should support it from day one.

## 4. Technology

**Booking platforms** [P/S]
- Mindbody: waitlist auto-promotion, "Clients at Risk" churn prediction, Messenger[ai] booking chatbot. (Source 51)
- ABC Glofox: configurable booking windows, cancellation rules, concurrent-booking caps, SMS and push waitlist notification, multi-location templates. (Source 50)
- Perfect Gym (LUF's platform): promotes ML churn prediction and club-occupancy forecasting. (Source 53)
- Virtuagym: describes demand forecasting from history, weekday, weather, events, seasonality and instructor popularity. (Source 52)
- Les Mills ClubCount and Connect: attendance capture, targets by class, instructor rankings, timetable publishing. (Source 10)

**AI and automation in GX operations, 2023-2026: the realistic state**
- Shipping today: waitlist auto-promotion, reminder automations, churn-risk scoring, member booking chatbots, automated sub routing.
- Mostly aspirational or vendor-claimed: AI timetable optimisation and per-class demand forecasting. No named large operator publicly states it uses AI timetable optimisation. Claims such as "AI booking lifts utilisation from 70-80% to 90-95%" are [U].
- Les Mills' stated AI direction is instructor support, not replacement: faster choreography learning, camera-based form feedback in practice, computer-vision class counting, attendance-pattern-based scheduling. (Source 15)
- AI class evaluation: no GX-specific product found. Adjacent tools exist in classroom observation and exercise-form analysis. Treat "AI instructor audit" as emerging.

What this means for LUF: the proposed agent is not chasing an unproven "AI timetable optimiser". It automates the proven, mundane layer (coordination, publishing, cover routing, payroll compilation, KPI reporting) that GroupEx PRO, NetGym and ClubCount sell as separate products, and adds a conversational layer on WhatsApp that none of them offer.

**Patterns**
1. Full-service clubs favour strike-based booking suspensions (3 strikes in 30 days leading to a 7-day suspension is the modal rule) over fees. Boutiques and franchises favour USD 10-20 fees.
2. Cancel windows cluster at 1-2 hours for full-service clubs, 8-12 hours for boutique and reformer formats.
3. Advance windows range from 26 hours (Equinox) through 2 days (PURE) to 6-7 days (Life Time, Fitness First AU, Celebrity) and 8 days (David Lloyd).
4. Waitlists close 5-60 minutes before class and auto-promote.

**How LUF's poster compares**: LUF's 77-hour window sits between PURE and Celebrity; the 2-hour cancel window is the full-service norm; the 1-hour online close is standard. LUF's "penalty email" for absence without cancellation is softer than the 3-strike suspension used by Celebrity Fitness, its closest Malaysian comparator. The agent can compute strike counts from PGM data so management can decide whether to adopt a formal strike rule.

## 6. Takeaways applied to Level Up Fitness

1. Kenny's 2022 instincts ("small number of classes, all full"; "retire a slot before adding one"; keep Les Mills programmes at 3+ a week) are the same rules Les Mills publishes. The agent should encode them, not replace them.
2. The single biggest practice gap versus SC Fitness and Les Mills ClubCount users is a **weekly scorecard** produced automatically from attendance data. That is workflow W4 in the proposal and should ship in phase 2, not later.
3. The industry standard for instructor evaluation is graded, not binary, and includes spot checks. Extend the QCC rather than rewrite it.
4. Cover management is a solved product category (GroupEx PRO, NetGym). The agent replicates the routing logic on WhatsApp so instructors need no new app.
5. Hybrid or scorecard-linked pay is now mainstream. Build payroll to support it.
6. Do not promise "AI timetable optimisation". Promise a reliable, data-backed weekly loop and recommendations a human approves.

## 7. Tooling research: AI in club operations, messaging, LUF's platform, backend options

### 7.1 AI agents actually deployed in club operations

- **Everything deployed today is member-facing.** No vendor or case study was found where an AI agent handles instructor sub coverage, weekly availability or roster confirmation. Staff coordination is still done with conventional workflow tools or human virtual assistants. An LLM-driven instructor coordinator would be novel. [S]
- ABC Fitness acquired Replify (July 2026) for agentic lead handling; earlier shipped an AI churn predictor and AI workout builder. Reported results (10x leads, 65% of call volume automated) are vendor claims. [S/U] (Sources 61, 62)
- **PushPress AI Assistant** (controlled beta late 2025, wider rollout 2026): plain-text prompts to manage plans and book recurring classes for review. No quantified results. [S] (Source 65)
- Les Mills corporate uses AI for churn-propensity targeting and instructor discovery (Les Mills Connect lists instructors by programme, location and teaching availability with verified certification). [S] (Source 74)
- Staff-side non-AI tools worth copying: **ClassSub** shows a manager "coverage health" (share of classes covered 24 h or more ahead, still-open requests) and per-instructor cover rate, confirmed rate and no-shows, and is pitched explicitly as the cure for "group-chat chaos". **GroupEx PRO** runs sub requests through an approval workflow with cost-per-head reporting. [S] (Sources 18, 19 in this section's list: 72, 73)

### 7.2 WhatsApp Business Platform vs Telegram for instructor coordination

**WhatsApp Cloud API, Malaysia** [S, several vendors agree]
- Per-message pricing since 1 July 2025. Malaysia base rates: marketing RM 0.35, utility RM 0.056, authentication RM 0.056 per template message. Replies inside a 24-hour service window are free today. (Sources 66, 67)
- From 1 October 2026: utility templates inside the 24-hour window become billable, and each business number gets 1,000 free service messages a month. (Source 68)
- Business Service Provider platform fees on top of Meta rates run roughly RM 150-1,200 a month (Wati about RM 299, SleekFlow about RM 469, respond.io USD 79). Going direct to the Cloud API avoids the fee but you host the webhook. (Source 69)
- Onboarding needs Meta Business Verification (SSM certificate, utility bill or bank statement in the company name, matching website and domain email). A number cannot be on both the WhatsApp Business app and the API, so a fresh number is normal. New accounts start with a business-initiated conversation cap that rises with quality rating. (Source 70)
- Hard rules: business-initiated messages must be pre-approved templates; explicit opt-in is required; free-form replies only inside the 24-hour window.
- **Groups API**: available only to green-tick Official Business Accounts, groups capped at 8 members, no add-participant endpoint, cannot read member-created groups. A 30-instructor broadcast group cannot be driven from the API, which confirms the one-to-one design. (Source 71)
- **WhatsApp Flows** (in-chat forms) suit weekly availability collection: multi-select days and slots, dynamic lists from the backend.

Cost sketch for 30-40 instructors: one weekly availability template, confirmations and ad-hoc cover broadcasts is roughly 300-600 utility messages a month, about RM 17-34 in Meta fees. The BSP subscription dominates unless LUF goes direct to the Cloud API.

**Telegram Bot API** [P]
- Free. Non-anonymous polls with multiple answers and auto-close, inline "Confirm" and "I can cover" buttons, works in private chats. Rate limits of about 30 messages a second bot-wide are irrelevant at club scale. (Source 75)
- Open-source precedents: an aiogram 3 bot using Claude plus PostgreSQL and Google Sheets sync with role-based access and a shift-management module [F] (Source 76); a weekly recurring reminder bot [F] (Source 77).
- Downside: instructors must install a second app. In Malaysia WhatsApp is near-universal; Telegram adoption is decent but not guaranteed.

### 7.3 Level Up Fitness's booking platform

**PushPress Platform API v3** (primary source: the TypeScript SDK README v1.15.0, April 2026, read in full [F]) (Sources 78-80)
- Base `https://api.pushpress.com/v3`, OpenAPI 3.1, `API-KEY` plus `company-id` headers (one key per location for multi-site gyms). TypeScript and PHP SDKs.
- **Read endpoints** that matter: `classes.list/get`, `classes.type.list/get`, `reservations.list/get` (bookings), `checkins.class.list/get` (attendance), `checkins.count`, `customers.*`, `enrollment.*`, `events.*`, `company.get`.
- **Outbound messaging endpoints**: `messages.email.send`, `messages.sms.send`, `messages.push.send`. The agent can notify booked members through PushPress itself.
- **Webhooks**: signed JSON for `checkin.created/updated/deleted`, `class.canceled`, `reservation.created`, `appointment.noShowed`, `customer.*`, `enrollment.created`.
- **Gaps that shape the design**: no write endpoints for classes, coach assignment or reservations. The agent can read the schedule and bookings but cannot create a class, reassign a coach or cancel a class via API. No waitlist endpoint found. Whether the Class object exposes coach and capacity is unverified (docs page blocked). Zapier and PushPress Grow add triggers ("Class Registered", "Late Cancel") but no "set coach" action.
- PushPress AI Assistant can already book recurring classes from a text prompt, which may become a semi-official publishing path if PushPress exposes it programmatically.

Consequence: **publishing the weekly timetable and assigning instructors stays a browser or manual step** until PushPress adds write endpoints. The agent prepares the exact change list; a human (or a Playwright script under the human's login) applies it in PushPress Core. Everything else in the proposal (reading, analytics, cover routing, member notification, payroll) is API-supported.

### 7.5 Instructor quality frameworks (detail)

- **Les Mills Qualifications (LMQ)**: five Key Elements (Choreography, Technique, Coaching, Connection, Performance), each graded 1-3 (Choreography 1-2). Grade combinations give a Level. Coaching is split into Layer 1 (clear what, where, how) and Layer 2 (motivational and educational). Assessment is a full-class video within 60 days of training, results in 14 days, three attempts. (Sources 37, 38 [F])
- LUF's Les Mills instructors already receive these grades. Aligning the club-side QCC to the same five dimensions means the club audit and the Les Mills assessment speak the same language.
- ACE core skills for group instructors: counting, cueing, demonstrating, offering modifications, motivating; recommends video self-assessment and post-class questions. (Source 86)
- AUSactive requires 20 CECs per 24 months plus current first aid for registered group instructors; observed sessions earn credit. CIMSPA publishes a Core Group Exercise Instructor professional standard (content not extracted). (Sources 87, 88)
- No AI class-evaluation product exists for GX. Form-analysis tools exist for individual exercise, not for a 20-person studio.
